================================================================================
  Dynamic Terrain — Fine Chunks
  Streaming local terrain at DEM resolution around the player
================================================================================
  Nov 2026 · companion to design.txt / surface.txt / movement.txt / scaling.txt.
  DESIGN DOCUMENT — stages 1-2 are not built yet. Stage 0 (the LOD dimension
  fix, commit 889e9c3) is done and pinned.

  The problem this document solves: on a true-scale body the document grid
  (f203 on Earth, 22 km hexes) CANNOT show towering relief — the data has
  real 1-3.6 km walls (verified by cross-section) but the grid averages
  ~360 DEM pixels per hex, and true-scale curvature eats what remains. The
  fix is not a bigger MAX_FREQUENCY and not a height blend. It is what every
  terrain engine does: render a FINE local region, streamed in ahead of the
  player and evicted behind while the body as a whole stays on its LOD chain
  in the sky.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE PROBLEM — TRUE-SCALE RELIEF HAS A HORIZON
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.1  The numbers (Earth, R = 6371.009 km = 181.09 u, 1 u = 35,181.6 m)

  The ground curves away below your horizontal line by the dip d^2/2R. A
  relief feature of height H at distance d is above your horizon only while
  H > d^2/2R — its "relief horizon" is sqrt(2 R H):

      dip d^2/2R:      35 km: 0.1 km     100 km: 0.8 km    300 km: 7.1 km
                       500 km: 19.6 km   1000 km: 78.5 km

      relief horizon:  H = 0.3 km (cliff face): 62 km
                       H = 1 km (foothill step): 113 km
                       H = 3.6 km (Sierra crest): 214 km
                       H = 8.8 km (Everest):      335 km

      a 3.6 km crest seen above local horizontal:
                       at 17 km:  11.9 deg   <- THIS is "towering"
                       at 35 km:   5.7 deg
                       at 100 km:  1.6 deg   <- "a low sliver on the horizon"
                       at 300 km:  below the horizon entirely

  The walker's eye (1.6 m) has a visual horizon of 4.5 km. "Towering"
  (>= 10 deg elevation) of a 300 m face requires standing within 1.7 km of
  it; of a 3.6 km crest, within 20 km. All of that happens in the NEAR
  field — the first ~350 km — and the rest of the visible disc is where
  that near field must not end abruptly.

1.2  The resolution floor

  The stored data is correct: the 37N cross-section across the Sierras is a
  real staircase (valley ~0.4 km to crest 3.0-3.6 km, 1-1.5 km steps per
  hex; land p99 neighbor step 1.1 km, max 3.7 km). But:

      f203 document:  412,092 hexes, edge 21.8 km, area 1,238 km^2
                      = ~362 pixels of the 1.85 km (1 arcmin) DEM per hex.

  Each hex height is the AVERAGE of 362 terrain pixels. The 300 m-1 km
  cliff faces that live at the DEM's native 1.85 km scale are averaged into
  the hex top along with the valley floor and the ridge behind. No LOD
  level can show them (a coarse hex adopts one fine cell's height — the
  averaging already happened upstream) and no blend weight can un-average
  them. The relief is not in the document. It is in the DEM.

1.3  Why not a bigger MAX_FREQUENCY

  A whole-planet grid at DEM resolution (hex edge = 1.85 km) is
  n ~ 2,395 -> 57.4 M cells: ~0.5 GB packed, ~800 M triangles (2.4 B
  vertices). The
  document grid is the PHYSICAL truth — mass, volume, anchors, persistence
  all derive from it — and it is the grid the geometry worker builds a full
  LOD chain of. 57 M cells is a dead end at the document level, and it
  would buy nothing: at no camera distance is the whole planet rendered at
  1.85 km. What is actually rendered at any moment is a REGION of the
  planet (the cap around the player) plus the LOD chain for the rest. So
  the fine resolution belongs to the REGION, not to the document:

      fine disc, D = 500 km:  ~88,000 cells  (~1.2 M tris)
      fine disc, D = 1000 km: ~353,000 cells (~5 M tris)

  trivial. The document stays at f203. The fine region is a presentational
  layer, exactly like the rings (bodies.rings: drawn, no physics, no
  document footprint).


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. THE CENTRAL PRINCIPLE — ONE SURFACE OF RECORD, NOW LAYERED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  surface.txt's invariant stands and is extended, not weakened:

    The canonical surface of a body is the RENDERED surface. Nothing that
    walks, picks, parks, lands, or reports altitude may resolve the surface
    to anything else.

  With fine chunks mounted, the rendered surface has two layers:

      inside the fine region (the union of mounted chunks):
          the fine chunks ARE the surface of record;
      outside it:
          the document mesh (its LOD level) is the surface of record.

  The two layers agree EXACTLY at the boundary by construction: fine cell
  heights fade to the enclosing document cell's height in a feather band at
  the region's outer edge (w -> 0 there, §4.3), so the fine region's edge
  sits on the document surface to sub-meter precision (the residual is the
  fine chord sag, ~0.1 m, against a seam 500+ km away). There is no visible
  seam, no floating ceiling, and no double surface — the document mesh is
  suppressed exactly where the fine region is mounted (§4.4), so the coarse
  fan can never poke through a valley the fine cells render below it.

  Every consumer dispatches to the surface of record at its position — one
  dispatch, never a mix:

      * the walker walks the fine fans where the fine region is mounted
        (it always is, under the player — the player is at the region's
        center, 500+ km from its edge);
      * the analytic pick (intersectHeightfield) runs against the fine
        chunk under the ray, and REMAPS the hit to the enclosing document
        cell, so applyBrush(planet.id, docCell) is unchanged (§6);
      * craft altitude (motion.js altitudeOf), the park anchor's r, the
        HUD's number — all read the fine surface while it is mounted;
      * a stroke on the fine region updates the document cell as today AND
        a sparse edit overlay on the fine cell, so both layers stay honest
        (§5).

  The document grid remains the PHYSICAL truth: mass/radius/volume, orbit
  elements, anchors, save format. The fine layer is derived (a pure
  function of DEM + document + edits, §4.2) and disposable — delete every
  chunk and the body is exactly as it was.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. THE FINE GRID — A BODY-FIXED CHUNK LATTICE, NOT A PLANET-WIDE FREQUENCY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.1  Why body-fixed chunks and not a moving disc

  The player's request is formulated: "as we traverse forward we load the
  next couple of kms worth of hexagons, and similarly forget the ones we
  just walked out of range from." A single disc that follows the player
  would have to rebuild its whole geometry every time the player crosses a
  fine hex — 88k-350k cells, ~0.6-2.3 s of worker time per trigger — and at
  craft speeds (844 km/s) it could never keep up. Instead the fine grid is
  FIXED TO THE BODY (in its spin frame, so it rides spin/orbit for free,
  like the document mesh) and is divided into CHUNKS; the player's position
  selects WHICH chunks are mounted. Moving across the surface then loads
  and evicts one chunk at a time — a chunk of ~4,000 cells costs ~0.03 s in
  the worker — and the loaded set slides in ~110 km steps.

3.2  The lattice

  * Fine hex edge a = the terrain catalog's pixel size (1.85 km for Earth;
    stage 1 runs the machinery at the DOCUMENT's hex size — §9).
  * The sphere is covered by two antipodal tangent patches (gnomonic from
    the body's local +Z and -Z). A cap of D <= 1000 km (<= 9 deg) sits
    wholly inside one patch; the player, at the cap's center, is always in
    exactly one. Projection distortion at the cap edge is ~1% — invisible,
    and it lives in the feather band anyway.
  * The "sits wholly inside one patch" clause has a finite range: at the
    patch horizon the gnomonic plane point diverges (plane radius
    tan(90° - γ) = 1/tan γ at horizon gap γ), and with it the §4.4 discard
    texture — the mounted set's bounding box spans
    2/(tan γ · chunkSide · a) texels per side. The required gap is
    γ = atan(2/(maxTexSide · chunkSide · a)) (maxTexSide 4096; finedisc.js
    `horizonGap`). A focus whose polar angle from its patch pole exceeds
    (90° - capAngle - γ) hosts NO region: the document surface renders
    there instead (stage 1: byte-identical; stage 2: the known cost of the
    two-patch scheme — §10.5's measurement decides the stage-2 encoding,
    which can lift the bound).
  * A chunk is a 64 x 64 fine-hex block of the lattice (~110 x 96 km),
    keyed (patch, i, j). Chunks tile each patch seamlessly: adjacent chunks
    share lattice rows EXACTLY, so the fine surface has no seams anywhere
    inside the loaded set. The only seam that exists is fine->document at
    the loaded set's outer edge, and that one is blended to zero (§4.3).

3.3  The loaded set

  loaded = { chunks intersecting the cap of radius D around the focus },
  where focus = the player's body-local position (foot: the walker; craft:
  the craft; EVA: the astronaut's dominant-body position). The cap is the
  coverage contract; the chunking is the streaming unit.

      D = 500 km:  ~21 chunks,  ~88k cells,  ~1.2 M tris,  ~0.6 s to build
      D = 1000 km: ~86 chunks, ~353k cells, ~5 M tris,    ~2.3 s to build

  (at the measured f203-chain rate of ~6.4 us/cell-level; both are trivial
  against the f203 document mesh, ~412k cells, already rendered while
  walking.)

  The worker queue is ordered by player intent, not by distance: chunks
  AHEAD of the velocity vector first (the player is always looking where
  they are going), the rest by distance, chunks BEHIND last. When a chunk
  ceases to intersect the cap it is evicted: GPU buffers disposed, DEM tile
  cache entries LRU'd. The loaded set is bounded by the cap, never by
  history.

3.4  What a chunk carries (per build)

  * the fine cells of the block: centers/corners from the lattice (the
    chord-fan geometry of §surface.txt, at fine scale), heights from the
    synthesis below;
  * cellByTri (inert fallback data, as today) and a FINE->DOC map: each
    fine cell's enclosing document cell index (4,096 x int32 = 16 KB) —
    this is what makes picking and painting hit the DOCUMENT (§6);
  * userData: chunk key, frequency (fine n), body id.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. HEIGHTS — THE FINE SURFACE IS A PURE FUNCTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.1  Synthesis

  For a fine cell c in chunk k at body-local direction dir(c):

      h_final(c) = w(c) * h_dem(c) + (1 - w(c)) * h_doc(enclosing(c))

  where h_dem is the terrain catalog's pixel under dir(c) (converted to
  world units via the world's scale — the same conversion that built the
  document from the same DEM, so the layers share a datum) and h_doc is the
  stored document cell's height. The edit overlay of §5 replaces h_final
  cell-by-cell where the player has painted.

  At stage 2 the fine hex edge equals the DEM pixel, so h_dem is the
  identity sample — no interpolation — and a wall is the DEM's true
  pixel-to-pixel step: the 300 m-1 km faces that the 362-pixel average
  destroyed.

4.2  Why a function of three inputs

  DEM, document and edits are the only things a fine height may depend on.
  That makes chunks reproducible (any chunk, any time, same bytes), evictable
  without loss (rebuild from the inputs), and safe to build in a worker
  (no shared mutable state — the same contract as the existing geometry
  worker, which imports the framework-free core unmodified).

4.3  The feather band (the seam killer)

  w(c) = 1 deep inside the loaded set, and fades smoothly to 0 across the
  outer feather — the loaded set's outermost ~20% of D (100 km at D = 500).
  Consequences:

  * at the fine region's edge, h_final = h_doc EXACTLY: the fine edge sits
    on the document surface, so the seam is invisible at any viewing
    distance (residual ~0.1 m fine chord sag, at 500+ km);
  * the fade happens where the curvature dip has already flattened the
    view (19.6 km dip at 500 km) — the last 100 km of the fine region read
    as rolling country and then continue as document country; no one can
    see the blend because the blend is doing the job curvature would do
    for free;
  * chunks keep their build until evicted, and every boundary chunk is
    built with the feather toward its outer edge, so the seam is continuous
    at ALL times — including mid-stream, while chunks are still landing.

4.4  Suppressing the document mesh under the fine region

  The document mesh (f203 while walking) is rendered whole; where a fine
  cell is BELOW the enclosing document average (a valley pixel under a
  362-pixel mean), the coarse fan would float above the fine surface — a
  ceiling over the walker. Rebuilding the document geometry with holes is
  out (a 3.5 s chain build per chunk change). Instead the document terrain
  SHADER discards fragments under mounted chunks:

      discard iff  (fragment inside the fine region's cap circle)
             &&   (the chunk containing the fragment is mounted)

  The fragment's position is already body-local (the mesh lives in the
  body's spin frame); the cap circle is two uniforms (center, radius, in
  the focus's patch tangent plane); "which chunk" is floor-divide
  arithmetic, and "is it mounted" is one lookup into a small data texture
  of the mounted-chunk set (<= 86 entries). O(1) per fragment, zero
  geometry cost, and the suppressed region EQUALS the fine region's extent
  exactly — no gap, no double surface, no stale hole. (The ocean disc is a
  smooth sphere at R and is NOT suppressed — it is the sea surface above
  the fine seafloor, continuous across the region by definition.)


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. EDITS ON THE FINE REGION — THE EDIT OVERLAY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  The open question from the mountain investigation, answered:

  A paint stroke on the fine region does FOUR things, in order:

  1. applyBrush(planet.id, docCell) — EXACTLY as today: the stroke hits the
     enclosing document cell (via the chunk's fine->doc map), so the
     document — and with it mass, the coarse views, the save, the physics
     anchors — stays the physical truth and stays consistent.
  2. records the stroke in the EDIT OVERLAY: a sparse per-fine-cell entry
     {fine key, h, t, c}. The overlay is tiny (only painted cells) and is
     part of the world's data (DB table for DB worlds, document field for
     file worlds — shape is an open question, §10).
  3. patches the MOUNTED chunk's vertices directly (a stroked cell is ~50
     non-indexed vertices) — same frame, so the weld invariant holds for the
     fine layer
     exactly as it does for the document (surface.txt: "the mesh and the
     surface re-derive from the new elements — in the same frame").
  4. re-synthesizes the affected chunk(s) in the background (0.03 s each,
     async, the old chunk stays visible) — the backstop that keeps the
     chunk's stored bytes honest after the overlay and the document moved.

  The reverse direction is free: a stroke on the DOCUMENT (coarse view,
  far view) changes h_doc for the enclosing cell, so any feathered chunk
  built afterwards blends to the new value; mounted boundary chunks get a
  background re-synthesis like (4). Edits compose: overlay > blend > DEM.

  What this is NOT: it does not write fine heights into the document (the
  document stays f203 — 57 M cells is a dead end, §1.3), and it does not
  fake relief procedurally (the DEM IS the terrain; a procedural
  micro-relief would be a lie the cross-section probe would catch).


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. PICKING, WALKING, ALTITUDE — THE DISPATCH
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  "One surface = the rendered surface" requires one dispatch point. A body
  with fine chunks mounted resolves its surface of record for a position p:

      surface(body, p) =
          fine chunk under p   if a chunk is mounted at p
          document surface     otherwise

  and EVERY consumer goes through it:

  * PICK: the analytic heightfield march (core/surface.js) runs against the
    fine chunk's local fan set. The fine chunk's cells are a lattice block
    with O(1) axial lookup from the ray's tangent-plane point — no trimesh,
    no spatial structure, no per-triangle scan (the analytic-picking
    invariant holds at fine scale). The hit remaps to the enclosing
    document cell (the chunk's fine->doc map) before anything else sees
    it — applyBrush, the selection, the brush preview all take a
    DOCUMENT cell, unchanged.
  * WALK: the walker's surface object is the fine surface while the player
    is on the body (the walker is at the region's center, 500+ km from the
    edge — it never crosses the fine->doc boundary, so the surface object
    never has to switch mid-stride). Crossing a CHUNK boundary is crossing
    two seamlessly-tiled lattice blocks: the fine surface is continuous
    there by construction.
  * ALTITUDE / PARK / LAUNCH: motion.js altitudeOf, the park anchor's r
    (terrain-resolved), and the launch pad all read surface(body, p) — so
    a parked shuttle's anchor sits on the REAL terrain (the DEM pixel under
    it), and "the terrain under the pad is real" becomes literal.
  * STUBS: fine chunks require the document (the blend input, the fine->doc
    map). The session already forces hydration before anything on-body
    happens — the chunks are downstream of it, never a bypass of it. A
    body without a terrain catalog gets no fine region: the document mesh
    renders as today, and nothing else changes.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. LIFECYCLE — WHEN CHUNKS EXIST AT ALL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  The hydration invariant (AGENTS.md: streaming only from user intent,
  NEVER camera-driven, NEVER at boot) extends to chunks verbatim:

  * MOUNT: a body gets a fine region when the player's SESSION is on it —
    foot, craft, or EVA with it as dominant body. Selecting a body,
    landing on it, a paint stroke at it — the same intents that hydrate.
    A free-cam fly-over does NOT mount chunks (the LOD chain is the
    free-cam's surface; mounting 2-5 M tris for a glance is the eager-
    everything behavior the hydration fix removed).
  * STREAM: while mounted, chunks follow the focus (§3.3): ahead-first
    streaming, behind-last, eviction at the cap's edge. One worker, the
    existing seq-supersede contract — a newer chunk job supersedes a stale
    one at its boundary, and the main thread keeps the previous chunk set
    visible until replacements land (the existing copy-on-write pattern).
  * UNMOUNT: the session leaves the body (launch, EVA drift away, lift-off,
    Esc to free-cam, selecting another body): the fine region unmounts —
    every chunk's GPU buffers disposed, the data texture cleared, the
    document shader's discard becomes a no-op (radius 0). The body is back
    to its LOD chain, byte-identical to a world that never had chunks.
  * DEM TILE CACHE: the worker holds an LRU of catalog tiles (~50 tiles,
    ~1.4 MB — a 1000 km cap touches ~100 tiles of 28.1 KiB each) so chunk
    rebuilds after eviction don't refetch.

  Memory, steady state (D = 1000 km, the big case):
      ~86 chunks x ~57k tris x 3 verts x 44 B  ~ 0.5-0.6 GB GPU
      (the same order as the f203 document mesh, ~380 MB, already resident;
      ocean chunks build fewer walls and cost less)
      + ~1.4 MB worker tile cache + ~16 KB per chunk of fine->doc maps.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. WHAT DOES NOT CHANGE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  * The document grid, element identity (positional, cells == 10n^2+2),
    MAX_FREQUENCY = 203 — it caps the DOCUMENT, the physical truth. The
    fine render grid is a different object and is not capped by it.
  * Mass / radius / volume derivation (planetMath) — still from the
    document. The fine layer contributes nothing to physics.
  * The LOD chain (lod.js) — a body in the sky renders whole at its
    minimum level. Stage 0 (889e9c3) made that true; chunks never touch
    it. The f203 document mesh keeps rendering the whole body; chunks only
    supersede it locally.
  * Physics — no cannon bodies for chunks (presentational, like the rings
    and the walker). The walker/craft stay kinematic.
  * The ocean disc (h 0 sphere), atmosphere, rings, starfield, labels —
    untouched. The fine water cells are seafloor BELOW the ocean disc, as
    today.
  * Serialize/save — the document serializes as today; the edit overlay
    serializes with the world (§5). Chunks themselves are never persisted
    (they are derived).

9. STAGES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  STAGE 0 — DONE (commit 889e9c3)
  The LOD dimension fix: level selection compared an R-normalized distance
  against world-unit thresholds, so giants never coarsened and "a body in
  the sky renders at its minimum level" never held. Pinned by the new
  headless LOD test (f12 -> f4 -> f12 with asserted camera distance).

  STAGE 1 — the machinery, at document frequency (engine only, NO new data)
  The whole architecture of this document, run with the fine grid at the
  DOCUMENT's own frequency (f203, a = 21.8 km, chunks 32 x 32 doc cells):
      * core/finedisc.js (framework-free): the two-patch lattice, chunk
        keys, the cap/loaded-set logic, the ahead-of-velocity queue,
        the chunk geometry builder (the document's cell builder, applied
        to a lattice block), the fine->doc map (the identity at document
        frequency — but built and exercised).
      * the worker: chunk jobs alongside chain jobs (same seq contract,
        supersede per chunk key).
      * Planet.jsx: chunk meshes mounted with the LOD level; the document
        terrain shader gains the §4.4 discard (data texture + cap
        uniforms); unmount = dispose + radius 0.
      * surface.js / motion.js: the surface(body, p) dispatch; walker,
        pick remap, altitude, park r on chunks; the four-step stroke
        (§5) with the overlay (an edit at document frequency is just the
        doc cell again — the overlay path is exercised, the DEM is not).
      * headless pins: the walker welds on chunks while crossing chunk
        boundaries; the pick remaps to the document cell; a stroke
        composites (doc + overlay + same-frame patch); eviction is clean
        (dispose, no leak, the doc mesh unsuppressed); unmount restores
        byte-identical LOD rendering.
  Visually a no-op (same data, same heights) — that is the point: it
  isolates EVERY mechanism (lattice math, tiling, streaming, eviction,
  suppression, dispatch, edit compositing) from the data pipeline, so
  stage 2 cannot hide a machinery bug behind a terrain reveal.

  STAGE 2 — the DEM (this is where the walls become towering)
  The data pipeline + the real fine grid (n ~ 2,395, a = 1.85 km):
      * db: terrain catalog table (migration 010) — (catalog, tile_lat,
        tile_lon, tile_size) natural key, int16 mm payload; the ETOPO2022
        1-arcmin grid (21,600 x 10,800 px, 933 MB float32 / 467 MB int16,
        16,200 tiles of 2 deg x 2 deg at 28.1 KiB) imported by
        `npm run db:terrain` (one-time, ~0.5 GB — DATA.md gets a row).
      * server: GET /api/terrain/:catalog/... tile endpoint (the app
        fetches tiles exactly as it fetches atoms — never raw postgres).
      * worker: the §4.1 synthesis with the real DEM and the real feather;
        the fine->doc map at fine scale.
      * constants.json: the dials — fineDisc.radius (D, default 500 km,
        range to 1000), the feather fraction — resolved like every other
        tunable; the shaders take float literals as always.
  The acceptance test is the one the user will run: stand on the Sierra
  crest in Sol Real and see 300 m-1 km faces at 1.85 km resolution —
  the real staircase the cross-section probe proved is in the data.

  STAGE 3 — OUT OF SCOPE (named so it can be picked up later)
  Other bodies with catalogs (the Moon: LRO; Mars: MOLA). User-authored
  fine terrain (a brush that writes fine cells directly — the overlay
  already has the shape). Fine-grid LOD (coarser fine levels for fast
  low flight — probably unnecessary: the cap already bounds the area).

10. DECISIONS (resolved before stage 1 — Nov 2026)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  1. D (the fine-disc radius) = 500 km, defined as the VISUAL HORIZON.
     Constant: tunables.fineDisc.radius — a PHYSICAL distance in km,
     converted per-body to world units via the world scale
     (D_u = radius*1000/scale). 500 km covers Earth's entire relief field
     (the Everest horizon is 335 km); a body small enough to hold the whole
     cap clamps it to its own horizon.
  2. Feather = the outer 20% of D, smoothstep.
     Constant: tunables.fineDisc.feather (0.2).
  3. Edit overlay persistence: a DB table for DB worlds + a document field
     for file worlds — mirrors the models/crafts split (P3).
  4. Chunk size = a fixed PHYSICAL footprint: the constant
     tunables.fineDisc.chunkKm (~118 km). The fine-hex count per chunk
     side is derived as round(chunkKm/hexKm) from the fine grid's hex size
     — 64 fine hexes at stage 2 (1.85 km) and ~5 doc cells at stage 1
     (21.8 km). A physical (not cell-count) size is what makes it one
     constant: at f203 the 500 km cap holds only ~634 doc cells, so the
     earlier draft's fixed 32x32 cell count would be one chunk bigger than
     the cap — nothing to stream. Fixed km keeps ~25 chunks in the cap.
  5. Suppression: fragment discard for stage 1 — then MEASURE (fragment
     cost vs the early-vertex-discard alternative, which would skip
     document walls at the region edge) before deciding on stage 2.
  6. EVA: CONFIRMED — an EVA astronaut whose dominant body is within D
     mounts that body's region (the walls under an adrift astronaut are the
     whole point of the view).
  7. HORIZON GAP: the region does not mount where the cap cannot sit wholly
     inside one patch with a bounded §4.4 texture — the focus's polar angle
     from its patch pole is capped at (90° - capAngle - γ), γ =
     atan(2/(maxTexSide · chunkSide · a)) (finedisc.js `focusSafe`; the
     document surface renders in that band — byte-identical at stage 1).
     Found stage 1: a craft driving a focus across a patch horizon made the
     plane point 1e9 and the texture box 1e20 bytes.

11. INVARIANT IMPACT (what this document changes about the engine's laws)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  EXTENDED (the law stands, with one word added):
  * "One surface = the rendered surface" (surface.txt) — the rendered
    surface is now LAYERED (fine chunks where mounted, document outside,
    continuous at the boundary by the feather). Every consumer dispatches
    through surface(body, p); no consumer may mix the layers.
  * "Picking is analytic" (AGENTS.md) — analytic at fine scale too (local
    fans + O(1) axial lookup), and it returns the DOCUMENT cell (remapped
    at the chunk) so the brush pipeline is unchanged.
  UNCHANGED:
  * hydration: only from user intent, never camera-driven, never at boot —
    chunks are downstream of the session, and a free-cam never mounts them;
  * element identity, mass/volume derivation, MAX_FREQUENCY (document);
  * the physics registry (60 Hz never through zustand; chunks are not
    bodies); the worker contract (one worker, seq-supersede, packed
    transferables — chunk jobs join the same stream);
  * serializeWorld strips nothing new (chunks are derived; the overlay
    serializes, §5); normalizeWorld's lenient healing extends to the
    overlay field.
  NEW:
  * a body's fine region is a derived, disposable layer: deleting every
    chunk must leave the body byte-identical (a test, not an aspiration);
  * the fine region's extent EQUALS the document mesh's suppressed region
    exactly (the shader's discard set == the mounted-chunk set) — a leak in
    either direction (a double surface, or a gap where neither layer
    renders) is a bug, pin it.
