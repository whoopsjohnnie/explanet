================================================================================
  ExPlanet — Scaling to a Realistic Solar System
  500k-hex Earths, generated giants, and dynamic scale
================================================================================
  Oct 2026 · DESIGN PROPOSAL — NOT FINAL. No code has been changed on the
  strength of this document. Companion to docs/design.txt (current design) and
  docs/landing.txt (surface mode). Everything below marked (measured) was
  observed in this codebase; everything else is analysis or proposal.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 0. STATUS & SCOPE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Status: DRAFT for discussion. Several genuine forks remain (see §9); the
  document is written to expose them, not to decide them.

  The question: now that worlds live in PostgreSQL and loading is staged
  (skeleton -> stubs -> streamed atoms) and rendering uses an LOD chain, what
  still prevents a realistic solar system where an Earth-sized world carries
  ~500k hexes? Larger bodies would then carry millions — or, proposed here,
  stars and hard-to-reach gas giants become FUNCTIONAL bodies: procedurally
  generated from a seed, never stored as atoms, because landing on a star has
  no point and paying storage for a Jupiter's surface has little of it.

  In scope: the atom-count coupling math, per-cell cost analysis, the
  generated-body model, the physics and camera-scale problems that are
  independent of hex count, and a phased path.

  Out of scope: rendering aesthetics (atmospheres, sun shaders), collision/
  destruction gameplay, multiplayer, and the UI design of the Add Planet
  dialog beyond what the data model forces.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 1. THE HEADLINE ANSWER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Nothing fundamental prevents it, and the "generate the giants, store the
  worlds" instinct is the correct escape hatch — but the blockers are not
  where intuition puts them:

  * The binding constraint is THE UNIVERSAL ATOM ITSELF (§2). Because one hex
    covers exactly one area unit on EVERY planet, cell count scales with
    SURFACE AREA. "Earth at 500k" is not an isolated choice: it silently
    mandates ~60M cells for a Jupiter and ~6 BILLION for a Sun. The
    MAX_FREQUENCY = 203 cap and the DB's CHECK (frequency BETWEEN 1 AND 203)
    are merely the tripwires that make this explicit. A realistic system with
    fine rocky worlds is only possible if resolution is decoupled per body —
    which is exactly what generated bodies do.

  * Most per-cell machinery survives 500k cells per body; a few specific
    things do not survive tens of millions (§3–§5). The worst offender is not
    storage, streaming, or the DB — it is that geometry is built as ONE
    monolithic mesh per LOD level and a single paint stroke rebuilds the
    whole chain for the whole planet.

  * The realistic-solar-system part has two blockers that have nothing to do
    with hexes at all (§6): the fixed-timestep pairwise N-body integrator
    cannot hold a real hierarchy of orbital periods, and float32 render space
    breaks at AU-scale coordinate magnitudes. "Dynamic scale" addresses the
    second; the first needs a real decision (Kepler mode vs adaptive N-body).


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 2. THE ATOM COUPLING (the math that drives every other decision)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Current invariant (design.txt §3): at scale = 1 one hex covers exactly ONE
  area unit on every planet; radius R(n) = sqrt((10n^2+2)/4*pi). Therefore,
  for a fixed atom size:

      cells  ∝ surface area ∝ R^2        and        frequency n ∝ R

  Anchor the atom at "Earth = ~500k cells" (frequency 224, 501,762 cells) and
  the true-size neighbours follow automatically:

  +-------------------+-----------+--------------+--------+-------------+-------------+
  | Body              | area/Earth| cells        | freq.  | grid bytes  | tile width  |
  +-------------------+-----------+--------------+--------+-------------+-------------+
  | Earth   (6371 km) | 1x        |      501,762 |    224 |      ~65 MB |      ~35 km |
  | Jupiter (69911 km)| ~120x     |   ~60 million|  ~2,460|    ~7.9 GB  |     ~3.2 km |
  | Sun  (696340 km)  | ~11,900x  |  ~6 billion  | ~24,500|    ~780 GB  |     ~0.3 km |
  +-------------------+-----------+--------------+--------+-------------+-------------+

  (grid bytes ≈ 130 B/cell, the flat typed-array layout of hexsphere.js —
  centers Float64 + corners Float32 + neighbors Int32 + sides + areas.
  "tile width" = corner-to-corner of the hex in km at that anchor; a 500k-hex
  Earth is ~35 km tiles, i.e. a large city per hex.)

  Consequences to accept explicitly:

  a) "Larger bodies in the millions of hexes" is optimistic by two orders of
     magnitude for stars. Under the universal atom, a true-size Sun is a
     6-billion-cell body. It cannot be stored, streamed, or meshed; not even
     its GRID can be materialized client-side (780 GB).

  b) Resolution is a SYSTEM-WIDE choice under the invariant. Raising Earth to
     500k re-anchors the atom (today's anchor is Jupiter = top grid; see
     starter.js KM2U = radiusForFrequency(MAX_FREQUENCY)/69911) and re-
     quantizes every body in every world, including saved files. This is a
     world-version bump, not a feature flag.

  c) The ONLY way to have fine rocky worlds and realistic giants at once is
     to stop requiring every body to share the atom size. Generated bodies
     (§5) do this by simply opting out: they carry their own hex scale. The
     alternative, which the starter world demonstrates today, is accepting
     that Jupiter's hexes are ~120x larger than Earth's — the same decoupling
     expressed through the ladder (Jupiter "fills the top grid" at ~412k).

  d) Mass is NOT a blocker. Mass = density x volume, volume integrates the
     heightfield; with atoms stored, realistic mass ratios come from the
     per-body `density` field. Quantization error at 500k cells is one cell
     in half a million — irrelevant. (For generated bodies see §5.4: radius
     stays derived from `frequency` even when no grid exists, which keeps the
     "never store derived data" contract intact.)

 3. COST ANALYSIS AT 500k CELLS (one large stored body)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Measured/derived numbers for THIS codebase at ~500k cells, and where each
  cost lands:

  SURVIVES FINE
  * DB storage & import. Row-per-atom at ~90 B/row (schema.sql note): ~45 MB
    per body, COPY-streamed. The schema comment's "comfortable into the tens
    of millions" holds; import is a batch job, not a UX path.
  * Picking. Analytic ray->heightfield march (surface.js intersectHeight-
    field); cost is LOD-independent and cell-count-independent. No change.
  * Walking. Surface mode integrates r(dir) = R + h(cell(dir)) against the
    heightfield; it needs a lookup, not a mesh. A 500k world walks fine —
    and note this means ground-level detail has ZERO mesh requirement (see
    the mesh budget, §4).
  * N-body cost. Pairwise physics depends on BODY count, not cell counts.
  * LOD machinery. Chain from 224 is [224,112,56,28,14,7,4]; sampling cost
    (sampleLodElements) is proportional to the COARSE grid, so fine levels
    cost nothing extra. Only the active level is mounted.
  * Copy-on-write paints. applyBrush copies the elements ARRAY (500k
    pointers, ~4 MB) — cheap in itself.
  * Stub/skeleton path. Radius/mass stay orbit-grade from `frequency` alone;
    planetVolume handles elements == null via stubAvgHeight. This machinery
    is body-size-agnostic and becomes the foundation of §5.

  COSTS NOTICEABLY BUT LIVES
  * Document elements as JS objects. elements[i] = { t, h, c } costs
    ~60–100 B + GC pressure per cell: ~50–70 MB of heap per 500k world, and
    EVERY hot loop walks it (planetVolume, planetStats, sampleLodElements,
    normalizeWorld, serializeWorld). The packed mirror (atomsPacked,
    ~15 B/cell) already exists for geometry; the DOCUMENT staying object-
    shaped is now the main memory line item. Survivable at 500k; it is one
    of the first walls past ~5M.
  * Hydration. fetchPlanetElements -> elementsFromPacked rebuilds 500k
    document objects ON THE MAIN THREAD (~0.5–1 s measured class of cost —
    atoms.js header already counts "400k small objects ~ 100 ms" per pure
    pack pass; the document-object build is the bigger share). Acceptable
    per-intent; must never become ambient.
  * Wire format. /api/bodies/:id/elements is gzip'd JSON with Float64 heights
    as TEXT: ~10 MB of text / ~3–5 MB gz per 500k atoms, JSON.parse'd on the
    main thread. Fine-ish at 500k; absurd at 60M (GB-scale text). The fix is
    nearly free because core/atoms.js IS the binary shape already — serve the
    packed typed arrays as an ArrayBuffer and skip JSON entirely.
  * Grid cache. getHexSphere memoizes per frequency in an UNBOUNDED module
    Map. At ~65 MB per 500k grid, a session touching a few large frequencies
    needs eviction (LRU of 2–3 grids is plenty; grids are cheap-ish to
    rebuild, ~125 ms at 412k (measured), and the worker can eat that).
  * Skeleton bake & previews. Server preview samples at freq-16 via core/lod.js
    — cost tracks the coarse grid. Fine.

  BREAKS AT 500k ALREADY (and this is the real engineering target)
  * MONOLITHIC GEOMETRY + WHOLE-CHAIN PAINT REBUILDS. Geometry is non-indexed
    triangle fans: 18 verts/hex at pos+col+uv (+normals) ≈ ~0.8 KB/cell. A
    500k world's TOP LOD level is ~3M triangles / ~400 MB of buffers, built
    and uploaded as single per-material batches per planet; the chain build
    scales linearly from the measured ~3.5 s at 412k, so ~4–5 s per build;
    and because element updates are copy-on-write ON THE WHOLE ARRAY, every
    paint stroke rebuilds the ENTIRE chain for the entire planet. Landable
    means paintable, so a 500k Earth ships with 4–5 s per brush stroke.
    There is also no frustum culling inside a planet: from low orbit you pay
    to draw the far hemisphere. This is THE blocker for large stored worlds,
    and its fix — chunked geometry (patch the icosahedron, mount/stream/
    rebuild per patch, dirty-patch on paint) — is the one real project in
    this document. See §7 phase 2.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 4. THE MESH BUDGET (what rendering actually needs)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  The instinct "500k cells needs 3M-triangle meshes" is wrong and worth
  writing down, because it changes what the project is:

  * LOD_SWITCH keeps a hex ~4–5 px at its level's retirement distance. At any
    camera distance only ONE level is mounted; to see full frequency 224 the
    camera must be inside ~LOD_SWITCH·R/224, i.e. skimming the surface.
  * Surface mode uses NO mesh — it is a heightfield walker.
  * Therefore the full-resolution mesh is only needed in a small neighborhood
    when the camera is close — exactly the patch-streaming model. A chunked
    renderer could keep a 500k-cell world at ~a few hundred thousand drawn
    triangles while never building the whole top-level mesh. Chunking is
    ALSO what makes paint rebuilds O(patch) instead of O(planet).

  Conclusion: cell count is not a rendering problem; monoliths are.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 5. GENERATED BODIES (functional / procedural, not stored)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  5.1 THE MODEL

  A generated body is a STUB THAT NEVER HYDRATES: no atoms rows, no wire
  fetch. Its surface is a pure function of (seed, generator params, requested
  frequency). Where a stored stub's hydration means "stream the rows", a
  generated body's hydration means "run the generator in the worker".

  Most seams already exist — this is the argument that the change is small
  where it counts:

  * Radius/mass without atoms: planetCellCount/planetRadius answer from
    `frequency` alone; planetVolume's stub branch integrates stubAvgHeight
    analytically; the skeleton already ships stubs with preview samples built
    by the app's own core/lod.js. isPlanetStub() is already the gate the code
    base agreed on ("never assume planet.elements exists").
  * The geometry worker already consumes PACKED typed arrays as transferables
    (core/atoms.js). A generator emitting { t, h, rgb } per level needs no new
    plumbing — it looks like hydration from the worker's point of view.
  * bodies.seed already exists in the schema.
  * The renderer mounts sampled LOD levels; for a generated body the
    generator produces the level DIRECTLY at that frequency instead of
    sampling a finer one (sampling is only an implementation of "get elements
    at frequency f"; for generated bodies it is the wrong implementation —
    you cannot sample a grid you refuse to build).

  5.2 THE FINE POINT: RADIUS WITHOUT THE GRID

  A Sun needs frequency ~24,500 to state its radius on the atom ladder — but
  getHexSphere(24500) is a 780 GB grid that must NEVER be constructed. Note
  the radius math itself never needs the grid (cellCountForFrequency is
  closed-form). So the rule is:

      A generated body may carry a NOMINAL frequency far above any
      constructable grid, and every code path that calls
      getHexSphere(planet.frequency) must be gated for generated bodies.

  That is an invariant to enforce (a helper like `gridMayBeBuilt(planet)`),
  and it is also a trap: surface.js, lod.js's sampleLodElements, planetVolume's
  non-stub branch, geometry builds, cellAtDirection — all of them touch the
  grid and all of them must respect the gate. This is the sharpest edge in
  the design and the main reason the stub-aware discipline in AGENTS.md
  already exists.

  5.3 ALTERNATIVE FORK: THE SCALE ESCAPE HATCH, REHABILITATED

  Instead of a nominal mega-frequency, a generated body could be:

      modest grid frequency (say 16–64)  x  scale (large)  +  generator

  i.e. take the "legacy escape hatch" (scale != 1, radius = grid radius x
  scale) and make it the NATURAL home of generated bodies: their hex size is
  set by scale, deliberately off-ladder, and the generator supplies relief at
  whatever resolution the viewer asks for. Nothing huge is ever constructed;
  walking (at grid frequency) and meshing become possible on gas giants if we
  want them; radius stays derived.

  The fork (decide in final design):
  * Option A (nominal mega-frequency): keeps "scale = 1 strict atom" pure for
    every stored body and pushes all generated bodies out of the grid
    business entirely; walking/meshing a generated body beyond preview LOD
    is off the table by construction.
  * Option B (generated x scale): one coherent mechanism (scale finally means
    "this body defines its own atom size"), everything remains possible, but
    "scale is legacy" in AGENTS.md/design.txt must be rewritten and every
    scale-sensitive path (relief scaling with h!) must be re-audited.

  5.4 MASS WITHOUT ATOMS

  The "radius/mass are never stored" contract survives both options: radius
  derives from frequency (x scale under B). Volume for a generated body comes
  from stubAvgHeight (already the stub contract) — and since the generator is
  a known function, its mean relief and relief variance can be computed once
  at creation (or analytically) and stored, exactly like a stub's stubAvg-
  Height. A Sun's mass is then density x analytic volume, with the full
  333,000-Earths ratio available through `density`, no 6-billion-cell integral.

  5.5 EDITING GENERATED BODIES: OVERRIDES, NOT HYDRATION

  Painting a generated body must NOT materialize 6 billion cells. The model:
  base = (seed, generator); edits = sparse overrides keyed by cell at the
  body's working grid; "materialize" = explicitly convert to a stored body at
  a chosen (capped) frequency. Until materialized, brush strokes either
  record overrides at the current view LOD or are dropped with feedback.
  DB shape: an atom_overrides table (or atoms rows with a per-body
  base='generated' flag) — details are §9 open questions, not decided here.

  5.6 POLICY

  * Stars: generated, never landable, never paintable. (planetStats already
    knows the terrain key 'star'.)
  * Gas giants: generated BY DEFAULT, walkable/paintable IF the design keeps
    them reachable (option B makes this cheap; option A forbids walking).
  * Rocky/habitable worlds: stored atoms (this is what the DB is for).
  * The default choice is a per-body document field, not a global rule.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 6. THE NON-HEX BLOCKERS (realistic-system physics & camera)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  6.1 THE INTEGRATOR

  Current physics (core/physics.js): zero global gravity, pairwise N-body per
  frame with fixed stepping and softening eps^2 = 4. That is correct for the
  current playground scale and WRONG for a real hierarchy: orbital periods
  span ~10^4 (Mercury 88 d vs Neptune 165 y); no single fixed dt integrates
  both without either the inner planet precessing into a spiral or the outer
  one crawling. Options, in rough order of honesty vs cost:

  a) Kepler mode: bodies with parent_body_id follow conic sections (closed-
     form, exact, free of drift), N-body retained for unpARENTED/free bodies
     and optional perturbation. The schema ALREADY carries parent_body_id as
     "an UI hint the N-body solver ignores" — this proposal makes it load-
     bearing. Matches expectations for a "solar system"; abandons true N-body
     chaos (acceptable: the current sim is already not Keplerian — softened,
     slightly precessing, by design).
  b) Adaptive substepping: symplectic/leapfrog with per-body dt (or shared
     dt driven by the closest pair). Physically pure, harder to keep stable,
     60 Hz presentation needs fixed substep budgets per frame.

  Either way: orbitalSpeed() must stay consistent with whatever force law
  wins (AGENTS.md invariant), softening's meaning changes when world units
  change meaning (eps = 2 units is ~800 km at the 500k-Earth anchor, but
  ~0.6 AU at a solar-system anchor — softening must be re-expressed relative
  to body radii or distances, not absolute).

  6.2 FLOAT32 RENDER SPACE

  three.js positions are float32. At AU-scale coordinate magnitudes: vertex
  jitter, orbit-line shimmer, and camera near/far dynamic range collapse.
  The proposal — "dynamic scale of the solar system" — is the right frame,
  and should be implemented as a VIEW-layer concern:

  * Document units stay atom units (document math never sees display scale).
  * The view composes: origin shift (render around camera / origin re-center)
    x display unit remap (a "system scale" chosen per world or continuously,
    e.g. AU<->px), with camera near/far derived from the active scale — the
    same trick Walker.jsx already plays with camera.near (§ AGENTS.md).
  * LOD_SWITCH distances are RATIOS (K·R/n), so they survive any uniform view
    scale untouched — LOD needs no change.
  * Live registry positions stay float64 (core/physics.js already keeps them
    outside React state); float32 enters only at the GPU boundary.

 7. PHASED PATH (each phase independently useful; order is a proposal)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  PHASE 1 — make a 500k stored world work end-to-end (small, boring):
    * Bump MAX_FREQUENCY (and bodies CHECK constraint, tests, docs). For
      stored bodies a cap at ~2M cells (n ~ 448) covers super-Earths with
      headroom; mega-frequencies belong to generated bodies (§5.2), not to
      the storage cap.
    * Binary atom wire: serve/fetch packed ArrayBuffers (core/atoms.js shape);
      keep gzip. JSON skeleton unchanged.
    * getHexSphere cache: bounded LRU (2–3 large grids).
    * Hydration: move document-object build off the main thread or make it
      lazy (packed-first document; see open question §9.1).
    * Test-quirk note: test-geometry/test-hexsphere loop 1..MAX_FREQUENCY —
      bumping the cap multiplies runtime quadratically; tests need a sampled
      strategy (spot frequencies + one large) BEFORE the bump lands.

  PHASE 2 — chunked geometry (the real project; prerequisite for >~1M):
    * Patch the icosahedron: geometry per (patch x LOD level); mount by
      frustum + distance; paint rebuilds only dirty patches; GPU buffers per
      patch. geometryService grows a patch-addressed API; the worker and the
      packed transfer model carry over unchanged.
    * This is also the frustum-culling fix and the paint-latency fix (§3).

  PHASE 3 — generated bodies:
    * kind/generation field in the document + schema; generator registry
      (stars: emissive noisy shells; giants: banded noise); preview bake
      from the generator at freq-16.
    * The getHexSphere gate (§5.2) + stub-aware audit of every grid-touching
      path; serializeWorld/save must NEVER hydrate generated bodies (they
      have nothing to hydrate — verify, don't assume).
    * Overrides-on-paint or materialize flow (§5.5); Add Planet / heal paths
      for the new body kind.

  PHASE 4 — realistic dynamics & scale:
    * Kepler mode (or adaptive substepping) per §6.1; softening re-anchored
      to body radii.
    * View-layer dynamic scale + origin shift per §6.2.
    * Starter: a true-scale-ish solar system (Sun + rocky worlds stored at
      100–500k cells + generated giants/star) as the showcase world.

 8. PER-WORLD UNIT SYSTEM — SCALE & PRECISION CONSTANTS (PROPOSED)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  PROPOSAL ONLY — no schema has been touched. Everything below is arithmetic
  on the §2 anchor ("Earth ≈ 500k hexes") plus standard astronomy constants
  (R⊕ 6371.009 km, area 510.07 Mkm², M⊕ 5.9722e24 kg).

  8.1 THE REFERENCE: A 32 km-HEX EARTH

  Hexes needed for a full-size Earth at ~32 km hexes:

      510.07e6 km² / (32 km)² ≈ 498,000 hexes
      → the geodesic ladder only allows counts 10n²+2:  FREQUENCY 224,
        501,762 HEXES  (the legal grid nearest "half a million").

  That grid fixes the hex geometry and the mass quantum:

      hex area        1,016.5 km²        (= sqrt: 31.9 km "size")
      hex side        19.8 km   (flat-to-flat 34.3 km, point-to-point 39.6)
      MASS PER HEX    5.9722e24 / 501,762 = 1.190e19 kg

  Scale of one atom of such a world: ~1.3% of Ceres (the dwarf planet is 79
  hexes), ~1/6,200 of the Moon, a couple billion Great Pyramids. A single
  paint stroke on a realistic Earth moves mountains in the literal sense —
  worth remembering for any future "terraform score".

  8.2 THE TWO WORLD CONSTANTS (and the derived third)

  What §8.1 computes for Earth generalizes to a per-world unit system with
  exactly two calibration constants — these are the proposed worlds-table
  extension:

    unit_km        real kilometres per one world LENGTH unit.  Because the
                   atom is one area unit, hex area = unit_km² exactly; this
                   single number IS the world's "scale and precision".
                   Earth anchor: unit_km = 6371.009 / R(224) = 31.883.

    unit_mass_kg   real kilograms per one world MASS unit, calibrated so a
                   reference-density (default 0.05) body gets its true mass:
                   Earth at freq 224 has m_game = 0.05 x (4/3)pi·199.822³
                   = 1.671e6, so unit_mass_kg = M⊕ / 1.671e6 = 3.574e18.
                   Game density then maps linearly to real density
                   (rho_game 1 ↔ 110.3 g/cm³): Earth 0.05 → 5.51 g/cm³ ✓,
                   Sun 0.0128 → 1.41 g/cm³ ✓.

  Two deliberate clarifications:

  * "Mass per hex" is a BODY property, not a world constant: at radius R the
    hex column is (1/3)·1·R game-units deep, so m_hex = m_body / cells(body)
    = rho_real · unit_km² · R_body / 3 — it grows with the body (a
    Jupiter-density column under a 32 km hex is ~10x Earth's per hex). The
    WORLD fixes unit_km and unit_mass_kg; per-body precision stays exactly
    what it always was: the `frequency` column.
  * Under the universal atom, "scale" and "precision" are the SAME knob for
    stored worlds — a finer unit_km gives every body more hexes. They only
    decouple for generated / per-body-scaled bodies (§5.3): there, unit_km
    calibrates the stored (landable) fraction of the world and the giants
    run their own hex sizes.

  The anchor ladder, for choosing a world's precision:

  +----------+---------+--------------+-----------------+
  | hex km   | freq.   | Earth cells  | Earth mass/hex  |
  +----------+---------+--------------+-----------------+
  |    128   |    56   |     31,362   |    1.90e20 kg   |
  |     64   |   112   |    125,442   |    4.76e19 kg   |
  |     32   |   224   |    501,762   |    1.19e19 kg   |
  |     16   |   446   |  1,989,162   |    3.00e18 kg   |
  |      8   |   893   |  7,974,492   |    7.49e17 kg   |
  +----------+---------+--------------+-----------------+

  Side effects of the 32 km anchor worth knowing: the Moon becomes a modest
  n=61 (37k hexes) and Ceres a chunky n=17 — believable atoms at believable
  cost — while Jupiter lands at n≈2,458 (60.4M cells), i.e. the §5 generated-
  body requirement in numbers. The 8 km row (~8M cells) is roughly where the
  "stored giant" path dies and generation takes over (§3 limits).

  The implicit THIRD constant is TIME, and it comes for free: G is already a
  per-world column (`gravity`). If the three (unit_km, unit_mass_kg, gravity)
  are set so gravity IS the real G in world units, the sim's time unit is
  forced: at the 32 km anchor with gravity = 40, one tick ≈ 2,331 s ≈ 39
  real minutes, so an Earth year ≈ 13,500 ticks ≈ 3.8 minutes of wall clock
  at 60 fps — a playable pace — and 1 AU ≈ 4.69e6 units. (The float32
  dynamic range at AU distances is still a §6.2 origin-shift problem; the
  unit system does not solve it, it just makes the numbers meaningful.)

  Proposed columns (NOT applied; shape only):

      ALTER TABLE worlds
        ADD COLUMN unit_km       double precision CHECK (unit_km > 0),
        ADD COLUMN unit_mass_kg  double precision CHECK (unit_mass_kg > 0);

  NULL on both = UNCALIBRATED world: today's feel-based playground semantics
  (gravity 40 "because it feels good", mass in abstract units). Existing and
  bundled worlds keep NULL; realistic worlds set both, and the UI can then
  display true km, kg and orbital periods. Radius/mass stay DERIVED exactly
  as always — these columns calibrate the units, they store nothing derived.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 9. OPEN QUESTIONS (to settle before this becomes design.txt prose)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  1) DOCUMENT SHAPE: should elements become packed-first (document stores
     typed arrays; the {t,h,c} objects dematerialized to an editing
     view)? At 500k cells the object document is the single largest memory
     item and the largest hydration cost; but "boring human-readable JSON"
     is a founding goal (design.txt §1) — a packed document saves the file
     but loses the hand-editable ideal unless the file format keeps objects.
     Likely split: wire + runtime packed, FILE stays objects (files are for
     small/hand-made worlds; big worlds live in the DB only).
  2) GENERATED-BODY FORK: nominal mega-frequency (Option A) vs scale
     rehabilitation (Option B), §5.3. Everything about walking/meshing
     giants hangs on this.
  3) STORAGE CAP: max cells for a STORED body (proposal ~2M). Determines
     the CHECK constraint, the Add Planet ladder, and test strategy.
  4) OVERRIDE MODEL: separate atom_overrides table vs atoms rows with
     base flag; override resolution at hydrate time (merge cost, paint UX:
     does a stroke land in overrides or force materialize?).
  5) PHYSICS FORK (§6.1): Kepler-hierarchy vs adaptive N-body. Also: does
     Kepler mode change what `isStatic`/gravity_immune means?
  6) DYNAMIC SCALE UX: per-world constant, continuous slider, or automatic
     with view distance? Does the document store anything (proposal: NO —
     pure view state, document units are sacred).
  7) WORLD FILES vs generated bodies: how does a generated body appear in a
     hand-written world.json? Proposal: { generated: { fn, params } } with
     no elements; normalizeWorld heals it; gen:world emits one for demo.
  8) What happens to the CURRENT starter world (atom anchor move, §2b):
     re-anchor everything, or ship the realistic system as a NEW world and
     keep the playground world at the current anchor? (Recommend: new world;
     bump document version; keep normalizeWorld lenient.)
  9) Unit columns (§8.2): confirm the NULL = uncalibrated semantics, and
     decide whether the skeleton/API should surface unit_km / unit_mass_kg
